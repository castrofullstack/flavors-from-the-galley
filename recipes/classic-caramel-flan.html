Classic Caramel Flan

Silky baked custard with a deep caramel sauce

Serves 8 to 10 Prep 25 minutes Bake About 60 to 75 minutes Chill At least 4 hours, preferably overnight

From my galley

Silky Custard, Deep Caramel

Flan is one of those desserts that looks considerably more complicated than it really is. The goal is a custard that is smooth, creamy, and just firm enough to hold its shape when unmolded, with plenty of dark caramel running down the sides.

The most important parts are cooking the caramel far enough to develop a slightly bittersweet flavor, keeping the custard gentle, and baking the whole thing in a water bath. The result is the kind of dessert that can be made a day ahead, turned out onto a platter just before serving, and looks pretty impressive with very little decoration required.

Ingredients

What Goes In

For the caramel

  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons light corn syrup

For the custard

  • 4 large eggs
  • 3 large egg yolks
  • 2 1/2 cups whole milk
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 2/3 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 teaspoons pure vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt

Directions

Caramel, Custard, Chill

  1. Prepare the oven and pan.

    Heat the oven to 325°F. Set a loaf pan, rectangular baking dish, or approximately 8-inch square baking dish inside a larger roasting pan. Put a kettle of water on to boil for the water bath.

  2. Make the caramel.

    Combine the sugar, water, and corn syrup in a heavy saucepan. Cook over medium-high heat without stirring once the mixture begins to boil. Gently swirl the pan occasionally as the sugar starts to color.

    Continue cooking until the caramel reaches a deep amber color. Don't stop at pale gold. That slightly darker caramel provides the bittersweet edge that keeps the finished dessert from simply tasting sugary.

  3. Coat the pan.

    Immediately pour the hot caramel into the baking dish. Carefully tilt the dish so the caramel evenly coats the bottom. Work quickly because it will begin hardening almost immediately.

    Set aside while you prepare the custard.

  4. Warm the dairy.

    Combine the milk, cream, and about half of the sugar in a saucepan. Heat over medium heat until steaming and the sugar has dissolved. Do not allow it to boil.

  5. Make the custard.

    In a large bowl, gently whisk the eggs, yolks, remaining sugar, vanilla, and salt until combined.

    Slowly pour the warm milk mixture into the eggs while whisking constantly. Avoid vigorous whisking. You're trying to make custard, not introduce air into it.

  6. Strain it.

    Pour the custard through a fine-mesh strainer directly into the caramel-lined baking dish.

    This small step makes a noticeable difference. It catches any bits of cooked egg and helps give the finished flan that exceptionally smooth texture.

  7. Create the water bath.

    Place the roasting pan containing the flan onto the oven rack. Carefully pour hot water into the roasting pan until it reaches roughly halfway up the sides of the flan dish.

  8. Bake gently.

    Bake for 60 to 75 minutes, depending on the depth and shape of your dish.

    The outer portion should be set, but the center should still have a slight jiggle when you gently move the pan. An instant-read thermometer inserted near the center should register approximately 175°F to 180°F.

    Don't wait until the entire custard feels completely firm. It will continue setting as it cools.

  9. Cool and chill.

    Carefully remove the flan from the water bath and allow it to cool on a rack for about 1 hour.

    Cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, although overnight is better.

  10. Unmold.

    Run a thin knife around the outside edge of the custard. Place your serving platter upside down over the baking dish.

    Take a moment to make sure you've got a good grip on everything.

    Then flip.

    Lift the baking dish slowly. The flan should release onto the platter as the caramel pours over the top and collects around the custard.

Galley tips

From the Galley

  • Don't fear dark caramel. The caramel should be considerably darker than honey before you take it off the heat. Pale caramel gives you sweetness. Dark amber caramel gives you sweetness plus complexity.
  • Keep the custard gentle. Vigorous whisking creates bubbles, and excessive heat can produce a rubbery custard riddled with tiny holes. Low heat and a water bath are what give flan its smooth texture.
  • Make it the day before. Flan is actually better suited to entertaining when made ahead. A full night in the refrigerator gives the custard time to set completely and allows some of the hardened caramel to dissolve into that sauce you see surrounding the flan in your photo.
