Pan-Seared Salmon Caesar Salad

Crisp romaine, a sharp homemade Caesar dressing, Parmesan, and a beautifully seared salmon fillet.

This is Caesar salad turned into dinner. The salmon gets a hard sear in a hot pan until the outside develops a deep, flavorful crust while the center stays tender. Serve it right over cold, crisp Caesar salad with plenty of Parmesan and a squeeze of lemon.

Serves 4 Prep 15 minutes Cook 10 minutes Total 25 minutes

From my galley

A Caesar Salad That Eats Like Dinner

This one goes back a while. I wanted Caesar salad, but I also wanted something substantial enough to call dinner. A piece of salmon and a hot frying pan solved that problem.

There isn't anything complicated about it. Cold, crisp romaine, a salty and lemony Caesar dressing, plenty of Parmesan, and salmon with a good crust straight from the pan. The contrast between the warm fish and cold salad is what makes it work.

It's also exactly the kind of food I like making in our little galley kitchen. No elaborate equipment, no professional technique required. Just a hot pan, good ingredients, and a few things I've learned through trial and error.

Ingredients

What Goes In

For the salmon

  • 4 salmon fillets, about 6 ounces each, skin-on or skinless
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges

For the Caesar salad

  • 2 large romaine hearts, washed, thoroughly dried, and roughly chopped
  • 1/2 cup freshly grated or shaved Parmesan
  • 1 cup croutons, preferably homemade
  • Freshly ground black pepper

For the Caesar dressing

  • 1 small can anchovy fillets, about 2 ounces, drained
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 1/3 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1/4 cup finely grated Parmesan
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Kosher salt, only if needed

Preparation

Dressing, Sear, Assemble

Make the dressing

  1. Finely mince the anchovies and garlic together until they form a paste.
  2. In a bowl, whisk together the anchovy-garlic paste, egg yolk, lemon juice, Dijon, and Worcestershire. Slowly drizzle in the olive oil while whisking constantly until the dressing thickens and emulsifies.
  3. Stir in the Parmesan and several grinds of black pepper. Taste before adding salt. Between the anchovies and Parmesan, it may not need any.
  4. If the dressing is thicker than you like, whisk in a teaspoon or two of cold water.

Cook the salmon

  1. Pat the salmon very dry with paper towels. Season with salt, pepper, garlic powder, and smoked paprika.
  2. Heat a heavy skillet over medium-high heat. Add the olive oil. Once the oil is shimmering, place the salmon in the pan. If you're using skin-on salmon, start skin-side down.
  3. Cook without moving it for about 4 to 5 minutes, allowing a deep golden crust to develop.
  4. Flip and cook another 2 to 4 minutes, depending on the thickness of the fillets and your preferred doneness. Finish with a squeeze of fresh lemon.
  5. Transfer the salmon to a plate and let it rest for a few minutes while you assemble the salad.

Assemble

  1. Place the romaine in a large bowl and add enough Caesar dressing to lightly coat the leaves. Toss thoroughly.
  2. Add the Parmesan and croutons and toss once more.
  3. Divide the Caesar salad among four wide bowls or plates. Place a warm salmon fillet directly over each salad. Finish with additional Parmesan, cracked black pepper, and a lemon wedge.

Galley tips

From the Galley

  • Dry salmon is the secret to the crust. Moisture on the surface creates steam, so give the fish a good pat with paper towels before seasoning it.
  • And don't drown the romaine in dressing. Start with less than you think you need and toss it aggressively. You can always add more.
